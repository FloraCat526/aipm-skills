# Problem Solving Frameworks

## 5 Whys

Use for root-cause analysis of a specific recurring problem.

Rules:

- Start with a concrete symptom.
- Ask why until reaching a controllable root cause.
- Validate each link with evidence.
- Do not stop at "human error" unless the system cause is identified.

Output:

| Level | Why | Evidence | Confidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

## Fishbone Diagram

Use when causes may come from multiple categories.

Common categories:

- People.
- Process.
- Product.
- Technology.
- Data.
- Environment.
- Measurement.

Output grouped cause hypotheses and validation actions.

## MECE

Use for clean decomposition.

Rules:

- Mutually exclusive: categories do not overlap.
- Collectively exhaustive: categories cover the whole problem.

Ask:

- What is the whole being decomposed?
- Are categories overlapping?
- What category catches edge cases?

## First Principles

Use when assumptions may be stale or inherited.

Steps:

1. State the goal.
2. List current assumptions.
3. Challenge each assumption.
4. Identify constraints that are truly fixed.
5. Rebuild possible solutions from fundamentals.

## Systems Thinking

Use for feedback loops, delayed effects, and second-order consequences.

Look for:

- Reinforcing loops.
- Balancing loops.
- Delays.
- Bottlenecks.
- Unintended consequences.

Output should include intervention points and risks of local optimization.
